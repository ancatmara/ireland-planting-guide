# Galway Garden Calendar: A Month-by-Month Plan

---

## 1. How to read this plan

**Plant references.** Each plant is named with the code from its section heading in the guide, for example *Apple (F2)* or *Hosta (S11)*. These are the codes on the plant headings (A1, B28, T36 and so on).

**What is from the guide and what is not.**

- Anything unmarked comes from the guide: its planting seasons, feeding advice, pruning notes, hardiness warnings and "West of Ireland" notes.
- **†** marks a timing or technique that the guide does *not* state, added from standard UK and Irish horticultural practice (RHS guidance and similar). These are sensible defaults. Check them against your plant labels and supplier's advice.
- Where the guide gives only a season (for example "spring"), the task is placed in the month that suits Galway conditions and marked it †.

**Coast or inland?** Galway gardens differ a lot. Where a task depends on frost risk, two timings are provided: **coast** (Galway Bay, Connemara and Clare-side shores) and **inland** (Athenry, Loughrea, Tuam and higher ground). Use whichever matches your garden, and lean towards the later date if your garden is exposed or in a hollow where cold air collects.

## 2. The Galway climate, and what it means for the garden

### What the data say

| Feature | What the sources show |
|---|---|
| **Temperature** | Maritime and mild. Galway city averages about 6.8 °C in January and about 16 °C in July (Galway Tourism). |
| **Rainfall** | About 1,100 mm a year in Galway city, compared with about 680 mm in Dublin. Rainfall in the west generally runs between 1,000 and 1,250 mm, and December and January are usually the wettest months. |
| **Wet summers happen** | Athenry had 165 mm in June 2025 (206% of its long-term average, its wettest June since 2012) and 297.3 mm in August 2019 (276% of average). Summer is not reliably dry. |
| **Frost, coast** | Historically, the coastal strip sees its last air frost in late March or early April, and coastal areas are generally frost-free until December. |
| **Frost, inland** | In the midlands the last air frost typically falls in the first half of May, and the first autumn frost arrives in October. A regional guide puts Galway's typical last frost at mid-April on the coast and late April to early May inland (indicative only). |
| **Cold snaps still happen** | Athenry recorded −7.6 °C on 9 January 2025 and had 28 days of air frost in winter 2024/25. Borderline plants inland are at real risk in a bad winter. |
| **Warm spells happen too** | Athenry reached 25.9 °C on 30 April 2025, the highest April temperature on record for Ireland. Early warmth followed by frost is a trap for early growth and blossom. |
| **Wind** | Mace Head on the Galway coast had the highest annual mean wind speed of Met Éireann's stations in 2025, at 26.9 km/h. Staking and shelter matter. |

### What this means in practice

1. **Drainage decides success or failure.** The guide's first rule applies all year: improve drainage before planting. Winter wet kills far more plants here than winter cold.
2. **Work the clay at the right time.** Never dig, plant into or walk on saturated clay. It compacts and smears, and it takes years to recover. A handful that crumbles is ready to work. A handful that rolls into a sticky sausage is not. †
3. **Two good planting windows.** Early autumn (soil still warm, rain returning) and spring (from late March, once the clay is workable) suit most plants. Midwinter planting into cold, waterlogged clay is the riskiest time. †
4. **Wind.** Stake dwarf-rootstock fruit trees permanently (the guide is firm on this), and stake tall perennials and annuals before they need it.
5. **Slugs and snails.** Mild, damp weather means they are active for most of the year. The guide names hostas, lupins and similar soft growth as the main victims.
6. **Fungal problems in damp summers.** Expect mildew (zinnia is named in the guide) and blight on tomatoes. Airflow, shelter from rain and prompt removal of affected growth help. †
7. **Frost is a window, not a date.** Treat all "after the last frost" dates as approximate, and keep fleece to hand until late May.

## 3. Standing rules (they apply in every month)

### Planting and soil

- **Planting hole:** at least twice the width and depth of the rootball. Mix the excavated clay 50/50 with multipurpose compost, then add 20–30% horticultural grit by volume.
- **Mulch:** horticultural bark, 5–7 cm deep, kept away from stems and crowns.
- **Test the soil pH** before planting. The guide notes that west of Ireland soils vary considerably. Retest each spring if you grow acid-lovers. †
- **Ericaceous plants** (blueberry, cranberry, lingonberry, heathers, camellia, skimmia, prickly heath, deer fern and similar) get ericaceous compost and ericaceous feed. Never add lime nearby.
- **Containers:** pebbles for the drainage layer, perlite at 10–20% in potting mixes, and grit-rich mixes for rosemary, lavender, thyme and alpines. Stand pots on feet so they never sit in water. †

### Pruning rules by type

| Type | Rule | Plants |
|---|---|---|
| **Stone fruit and ornamental *Prunus*** | **Summer only.** Winter wounds invite silver leaf and bacterial canker. | Almond (F1), Plum (F8), Plum 'Aprimira' (F17), Peach (F15), Sour Cherry (F24), Sweet Cherry (F26), Japanese Flowering Cherry (T24) |
| **Apple and pear** | Winter, while dormant. Trained forms (espalier, cordon) in summer. | Apple (F2) †, Pear (F16) |
| **Spring-flowering shrubs** | Straight after flowering, never in autumn or winter. | Forsythia (T5), Deutzia (T15), Mock Orange (T28), Chocolate Vine (C2), Schisandra (C13, lightly), Common Jasmine (C7) |
| **Hedges** | Between 1 September and the end of February, because of the nesting-season ban (see below). | Hawthorn (T7), Holly (T18), Irish Yew (T20), Japanese Barberry (T21) |
| **Deciduous ornamental grasses** | Cut hard in late winter, before new growth. | Dwarf Fountain, Feather Reed, Japanese Blood Grass and Pampas (P28) |
| **Evergreen grasses** | Comb out or trim lightly in spring. Never cut hard back. | Blue Fescue, Mexican Feather Grass, Japanese Forest Grass, Evergold Sedge (P28) |

**The nesting-season ban.** Under Section 40 of the Wildlife Act 1976 (as amended), it is an offence to cut, burn or otherwise destroy hedge vegetation between **1 March and 31 August**, with limited exemptions such as road safety. BirdWatch Ireland states that the ban applies to private gardens as much as to farms. Do all hedge cutting in September to February. Check any dense shrub or hedge for nests before pruning in spring or summer.

### Feeding rules

- Most feeds should stop by **midsummer to August**, so that wood ripens before winter. The guide gives specific stop dates: apple (after July), plum and peach (midsummer), wisteria (midsummer), Japanese maple (after July), roses and clematis (late August), hosta and kiwi (August), hydrangea (August), camellia (midsummer).
- Tomato feed is the guide's standard high-potash feed for fruiting and flowering plants.
- Avoid high-nitrogen feeds on plants grown for flowers or fruit. The guide repeats this for raspberries on wet clay, wisteria, clematis, honeysuckle, lilac and others.

### Safety

- **Wear gloves** for Colchicum corms (B18, highly toxic), Monkshood (S13), Winter Aconite (B29), Yellow Flag Iris (W25), Water Crowfoot (W7), Chinese Lantern (P100), and thorny plants: Sea Buckthorn (F22) and Japanese Barberry (T21).
- **Keep away from children, pets and livestock:** holly berries, Irish yew, snowdrops, daffodils, bluebells, fritillaria, monkshood, lily of the valley and the other plants in the guide's toxic list.

## 4. Points in the guide to check before you rely on it

1. **Water Hyacinth (W100) is excluded from this plan.** The guide states it is banned and must not be bought or grown.
2. **Fringed Water Lily (W10) is legally restricted in the Republic of Ireland as well.** The guide mentions only Northern Ireland. In my searches, the National Parks and Wildlife Service's list of Third Schedule species (S.I. 477/2011) includes *Nymphoides peltata*, and Regulation 49 makes it an offence to plant, disperse or cause such a plant to grow. One plant retailer says the listing does not stop gardeners growing it, so the sources disagree. I am not a lawyer. My advice is **not to plant it**, and to check with the NPWS if you already have it.
3. **Water Soldier (W28):** it appears in a Northern Ireland schedule of invasive non-native plants. I did not find it on the Republic's list in my searches, but I did not check the full list. Check before planting, and never let any escape into open water.
4. **Plum 'Aprimira' (F17) contradicts itself.** It says to prune only in summer and never in winter, but also to prune lightly each February. This plan follows the summer-only rule, because of silver leaf.
5. **Cowslip (S5) repeats the Auricula (S2) text**, which describes alpine primulas needing pots or raised troughs. Cowslip is a meadow primula. † Treat it as a tolerant garden plant in reasonably drained ground. The plan gives it the guide's one stated task, which is dividing after flowering.
6. **Mealycup Sage 'Blue Bedder' (A12)** has text copied from the Mediterranean herb entries. † It is a tender perennial normally grown as a half-hardy annual, so the plan treats it that way.
7. **Plant codes differ** between the plant headings and the reference table (see section 1).
8. **Tender plants described as "borderline"** (Agapanthus, Eucomis, Society Garlic, Pickle Plant, Golden Mimosa, Lemon Verbena and others) are at most risk inland. Athenry reached −7.6 °C in January 2025. The plan treats them as containers that can be moved under cover unless you have a very sheltered coastal garden.

## 5. The monthly plan

---

### JANUARY

**Galway conditions.** The coldest and one of the wettest months. Mean temperature is about 6–7 °C, daylight is short, and the clay is usually saturated. Inland frosts can be hard (Athenry fell to −7.6 °C in January 2025). This is a month for protecting, pruning and planning, not for digging.

**Priorities:** (1) stay off wet clay, (2) check stakes and winter protection after gales, (3) prune what is meant to be pruned now, and (4) check stored tubers and overwintering pots.

**Prune and tidy (dry, frost-free days only)**
- **Apple (F2) †** and **Pear (F16):** winter prune while dormant. Trained forms such as espalier and cordon are pruned in summer instead.
- **Wisteria (C16) †:** shorten the shoots you cut back last summer to two or three buds (RHS: January or February).
- **Climbing roses (C6) and shrub roses (T36) †:** tie in climbers and remove dead and crossing wood. Hold the main prune of shrub roses until February or March.
- **Hardy Grapes (C8) †:** prune now while fully dormant, ideally before the sap rises.
- **Hazelnut (F11):** traditional late-winter pruning. Finish by February.
- **Do not prune** any *Prunus*: Almond (F1), Plum (F8, F17), Peach (F15), cherries (F24, F26, T24). The guide says summer only.
- **Do not prune** Forsythia (T5), Deutzia (T15), Mock Orange (T28) or Lilac (T29 †) now. They flower on old wood, so wait until they have flowered.
- **Witch Hazel (T41):** leave alone while it flowers.

**Protect, check and store**
- **Check every stake and tie** on dwarf-rootstock fruit trees (Apple F2, Plum F8, Pear F16) and young trees such as Black Locust (T4). Permanent staking is essential against Atlantic winds.
- **Tree ferns** (Tasmanian S23, Rough S18, Silver S20): keep the crown wrapped with fleece or straw in hard frost. Check that the wrap is not sodden.
- **Borderline plants left in the ground:** keep thick dry mulch over Pineapple Lily (B21), Agapanthus (B2), River Lily (B23), Alstroemeria (P1), Society Garlic (P41) and Dwarf Sword Lily (B9), where you have left them in.
- **Stored tender bulbs and tubers** (Acidanthera B1, Ixia B5, Freesia B11, Iron Cross B15, Ranunculus B22, Dwarf Sword Lily B9): look for mould or shrivelling. Keep them dry and frost-free. †
- **Overwintering pots under cover:** Lemon Verbena (H17, keep on the dry side), Lemongrass (H18), Norfolk Island Pine (T34, bright and cool at about 17 °C, no feeding), and any Olive (F14), Pomegranate (F18) or Golden Mimosa (T17) in containers. Water sparingly.
- **Pots outdoors:** make sure they stand on feet and are not full of water. Wet roots kill borderline plants faster than frost does.

**Enjoy** Winter Aconite (B29, January to February), Snowdrops (B25), Cyclamen coum (B27, midwinter), Hellebore (S10), Witch Hazel (T41), Winter Heath (G6, *Erica carnea*) and the first Golden Mimosa (T17) in a mild spot.

**Pond.** If the pond freezes, melt a small hole with a pan of hot water so gases can escape. Do not hammer the ice. †

**Plan and order.** Check seed stocks. Order tubers and corms for spring planting (Ranunculus B22, Acidanthera B1, Freesia B11, Ixia B5). Order bare-root fruit and roses if you have not yet planted them.

**If it is too wet.** Do nothing to the soil. Pruning, tidying, repairs and ordering all work in the rain.

### FEBRUARY

**Galway conditions.** Still wet and cool (mean about 6 °C), but the days are noticeably longer. The first dry spells can arrive. Early blossom is already at risk.

**Priorities:** (1) the February fertiliser round for fruit trees, (2) finishing winter pruning, (3) cutting back old foliage, and (4) first sowings under cover.

**Feed (all as stated in the guide)**
- **Balanced fruit-tree fertiliser (Growmore or a specialist feed) every February:** Apple (F2), Garden Plum (F8), Almond (F1), Peach (F15) and Pear (F16). Plum 'Aprimira' (F17) in the ground is also fed each February. Apple also gets bonemeal in the planting hole at planting.
- **Sulphate of potash in late winter:** Redcurrant (F20) and Gooseberry (F9, late winter or early spring).
- **Conifer feed:** Juniper 'Repanda' (G11) and Dwarf Mountain Pine (T10) get an occasional dressing of Kieserite or organic conifer feed in February or March. Newly planted ones wait 6–8 weeks after planting.

**Prune and tidy**
- **Kiwi 'Jenny' (C11):** late-winter pruning to establish the framework.
- **Fig (F7):** shape in late winter or early spring.
- **Elder 'Black Lace' (T11):** for the biggest, darkest leaves, cut hard back to a framework now (this sacrifices some flowers). Prune more lightly if you want flowers and berries.
- **Epimedium (S7):** cut old, tatty foliage hard back just before the flower spikes emerge.
- **Hellebore (S10):** clear old foliage before the early-spring feed.
- **Deciduous ornamental grasses (P28):** cut hard back to a few centimetres above the ground before new growth starts. **Pampas Grass:** cut back hard now or in early spring, wearing thick gloves and long sleeves.
- **Wisteria (C16) †:** finish the winter prune by the end of February.
- **Raspberry (F19) †:** if yours are *autumn-fruiting*, cut all canes to the ground now. Summer-fruiting types are pruned after harvest (August), so leave them.
- **Blueberry (F5) †:** a light late-winter tidy, removing the oldest and weakest stems.
- **Shrub and climbing roses (T36, C6) †:** main prune from mid-February into March.
- **Hedges (T7, T18, T20, T21):** last chance before the ban begins on 1 March. Finish all trimming this month.
- **Ivy (C10) and Virginia Creeper (C15) †:** trim back now, before nesting begins.

**Sow and plant (under cover or in a frost-free spot †)**
- **Ranunculus (B22):** plant in pots from February to April. Soak the tubers for 2–4 hours first, with the claws pointing down, 5–8 cm deep.
- **Sweet Peas (A19):** sow in pots under cover. † The guide allows spring or autumn sowing.
- **Garlic (H13):** if you started cloves in modules in a cold frame over winter (the guide's suggestion for heavy clay), plant them out in early spring when the soil is workable.
- **Snowdrops "in the green" (B25) and Winter Aconite (B29):** buy and plant just after flowering, with the leaves still on. This is more reliable than dry bulbs.

**Protect**
- **Almond (F1)** flowers as early as February, so blossom is very vulnerable. Have fleece ready for frosty nights.
- **Magnolias (T30), Peach (F15) and Plum (F8)** will follow in March and April. Avoid east-facing positions where morning sun hits frosted flowers.

**If it is too wet.** Prune, order, tidy pots and sharpen tools. Do not plant into saturated clay.

### MARCH

**Galway conditions.** Mean about 7–8 °C. Soils warm slowly, and clay stays cold and wet for much of the month. Frosts remain possible on the coast until early April and inland into May. Wind and heavy showers are common. **The hedge-cutting ban starts on 1 March.**

**Priorities:** (1) first plantings and sowings once the clay is workable, (2) the main spring feed, (3) the last big pruning jobs, and (4) slug defence from the first shoots.

**Sow and plant**
- **Cornflower, Poppies, Pot Marigold, Poached Egg Plant, California Poppy, Love-in-a-Mist and Quaking Grass** (A5, A14, A15, A13, A3, A11, A9): direct sow where they are to flower once the soil is workable (March in a mild coastal garden, April on cold inland clay †). Poppies need light, so barely cover the seed. California Poppy and Love-in-a-Mist dislike root disturbance.
- **Half-hardy annuals under cover:** Cockscomb (A4), Globe Amaranth (A8), Lambs Tails (A100), Strawflower (A17), Zinnia (A21), Snapdragons (A16), Drumstick Flower (A7), Winged Everlasting (A20). Sow in March or April † and plant out only after frost risk has passed.
- **Basil (H2):** sow under cover. Keep it in pots on a warm sill, patio or in a greenhouse, because direct sowing outdoors is unreliable here.
- **Peas (H24):** on heavy, cold clay the guide recommends starting seed in modules under cover. Sow successionally every few weeks from now into early summer.
- **Radish, Rocket, Spring Onions, Green Onions, Mustard Greens** (H26, H27, H101, H14, H100): first sowings under a cloche or fleece † as the soil allows. Garden Cress (H12) can be sown on a windowsill.
- **Ragged Robin (W20):** surface-sow in March or April.
- **Asparagus (H1) †:** plant crowns in a trench 15–20 cm deep in a raised bed, backfilling gradually as shoots grow. Harvest nothing in years one and two.
- **Rhubarb (F21) †:** plant or divide crowns with buds 1–2 cm below the soil.
- **Strawberries (F25) †:** plant with the crown exactly at soil level (too deep rots, too shallow dries).
- **Ixia (B5) and Freesia (B11):** plant in pots in March or April, 8–10 cm and 5–8 cm deep respectively.
- **Lilies (B16, B17):** the guide allows early-spring planting, 15 cm deep on a bed of grit.
- **Horseradish (H102):** root cuttings or crowns in spring, in a large, deep container to control its spread.
- **Hops (C9) †:** plant rhizomes 5–8 cm deep.
- **Chives (H5), Mint (H19, H25):** divide chives every 2–3 years. Repot mint into fresh compost each year (guide) and keep it in a container at least 30 cm wide and deep. †

**Prune and tidy**
- **Roses (T36, C6) †:** finish pruning by mid-March, before the first feed.
- **Clematis (C3) †:** Group 3 (late-flowering) cut hard to a pair of buds near the base. Group 2 (large-flowered early) tidy lightly and remove dead stems. Group 1 (including *C. montana*) wait until after flowering. Evergreen types need only tidying after flowering.
- **Witch Hazel (T41):** once it has flowered, remove only dead, diseased or crossing wood. Avoid hard pruning.
- **Eucalyptus (T6) †:** the guide says gardeners stool or coppice every year or two to keep the rounded juvenile foliage. Late March to April is the usual time.
- **Bamboo (T3) †:** remove dead and old canes.
- **Grasses (P28):** finish cutting back deciduous types. Comb evergreen types lightly.
- **Heathers (G6) †:** shear *Calluna* in March or April, just below the old flower heads and never into bare wood. *Erica carnea* is trimmed after flowering.
- **Passion Flower (C1) †:** if frost has cut it back (the guide says this can happen), wait for new growth to show before removing dead stems.
- **Do not cut** hedges, or vegetation in dense shrubs where birds may nest, from now until September.

**Feed and mulch (guide timings)**
- **Roses (T36, C6):** specialist rose fertiliser in late March.
- **Early-spring balanced fertiliser (Growmore or similar):** Blackberries (F3), Raspberry (F19), Strawberry (F25), Serviceberry (F23), Honeyberry (F12), Forsythia (T5), Deutzia (T15), Mock Orange (T28), Japanese Flowering Cherry (T24), Sweet and Sour Cherry (F26, F24), Lilac (T29, superphosphate or balanced), Clematis (C3, slow-release), Kiwi (C11, as buds swell), Honeysuckle (C4), Hardy Grapes (C8, balanced with potassium), Rhubarb (F21), Delphiniums (P12), Hosta (S11, slow-release), Hellebore (S10, slow-release), Creeping Phlox (G4, slow-release).
- **High-nitrogen fertiliser:** Blackcurrant (F4) in early spring, and Bamboo (T3) and Hops (C9) each spring.
- **Ericaceous feed:** Heathers (G6) in March or April, and Camellia (T22) after flowering. Skimmia (T26) and Prickly Heath (G15) in spring.
- **Peonies (P31):** balanced fertiliser as the red shoots emerge.
- **Magnolias (T30):** balanced or ericaceous slow-release in spring.
- **Mulch** with bark or compost once the soil has warmed a little and is moist, ideally in April on cold clay. †

**Protect**
- **Slug defence from the first shoots:** Hosta (S11, wool pellets, copper tape on pots or nematodes), Lupins (P26, protect emerging growth), Delphiniums (P12 †).
- **Blossom frost cover:** Peach (F15) blossoms March to April, so protect with fleece on frosty nights. Magnolias (T30) and plums (F8, F17) are also at risk.
- **Cats and young Catnip (H3):** a cloche or wire cage protects new plants.
- **Tree ferns (S23, S18, S20):** leave the wrap on until the hard frosts are clearly over, then unwrap gradually. †

**Divide and move (when the clay is crumbly) †:** Hostas (S11), Daylilies (P11, every 3–4 years), Avens (P2, P7, every 2–3 years), Heuchera (S4, every 3 years, spring or autumn), Helenium (P39), Bearded Iris (P19, hold until after flowering).

**Enjoy** Daffodils (B7), Crocus (B6), Hyacinth (B14), Dwarf Iris (B8), Siberian Squill (B24), Marsh Marigold (W17), Umbrella Plant (W23, March to April), Almond (F1) and Peach (F15) blossom, and Magnolias (T30).

**If it is too wet.** Sow in modules under cover, and prune, tie in and feed. Wait to plant out until the clay crumbles in the hand.

### APRIL

**Galway conditions.** Mean about 9–10 °C. The soil is warming and growth takes off, but frost is not finished: coastal gardens usually lose it by mid-April, inland gardens often not until early May. April can swing from record warmth (25.9 °C at Athenry on 30 April 2025) to hard night frost, so keep fleece handy.

**Priorities:** (1) the main planting month for hardy plants in the ground, (2) sowing, (3) starting the regular feeding schedule, and (4) pruning spring-flowering shrubs as soon as the flowers fade.

**Sow and plant**
- **Hardy annuals, direct:** Borage (A1, 1 cm deep), Sweet Alyssum (A18, 3 mm), Phacelia (A10, spring through summer, for pollinators or as a green manure), Poached Egg Plant (A13), Cornflower (A5), Pot Marigold (A15) and Poppies (A14).
- **Sweet Peas (A19):** plant out hardened-off plants against canes, a trellis or wigwam. †
- **Perennial vegetables:** Asparagus crowns (H1) and Rhubarb (F21) if not already planted.
- **Peas (H24), Radish (H26), Rocket (H27), Spring Onions (H101, H14), Mustard Greens (H100), Parsley (H22 †), Coriander and Dill (H8, H11):** sow in short rows every few weeks. Coriander and Dill bolt if overfed, so feed little.
- **Tender bulbs, in pots or the ground after frost risk (coast: now; inland: late April to May):** Acidanthera (B1, 10–16 cm on a bed of sharp sand, April to May), Iron Cross (B15, 5 cm, April to May), Dwarf Sword Lily (B9, 10–16 cm), Ixia (B5) and Freesia (B11) if not yet planted, and Pineapple Lily (B21, 10–15 cm, after frost).
- **River Lily (B23):** plant rhizomes at soil level in spring.
- **Kiwi 'Jenny' (C11):** plant in April or May once frost risk has passed. Give it a sheltered, sun-warmed wall.
- **Japanese Anemone (P20):** plant potted rhizomes in spring. **Siberian Iris (P19):** at soil level or 2–3 cm below, in spring or early autumn.
- **Container fruit:** Blueberry (F5), Cranberry (F6) and Lingonberry (F13) in ericaceous compost with plenty of perlite and drainage. Pot on or refresh the top layer now. †
- **Lawn alternatives:** White Clover (G22) can be sown direct, and Chamomile (H4) seed is surface-sown in spring.
- **Pond (from late April): † plant marginals** such as Marsh Marigold (W17), Bogbean (W5), Flowering Rush (W9), Arrowhead (W1), Mare's Tail (W16, in a basket), Yellow Flag Iris (W25, in a basket), Water Forget-me-not (W24) and Bog Pimpernel (W4). Push **aquatic fertiliser tablets** into the basket compost at the start of the growing season.
- **Water Violet (W29):** plant in spring or autumn, then leave it undisturbed through summer.

**Prune and tidy**
- **Forsythia (T5):** prune immediately after flowering, then mulch. Never prune in autumn or winter.
- **Hydrangea macrophylla (T32) †:** remove last year's flower heads down to a pair of healthy buds once the worst frosts are over. Leave the old heads on until then, as frost protection.
- **Heathers (G6):** finish shearing *Calluna*. Trim *Erica carnea* straight after flowering. †
- **Rockcress (G17):** trim lightly after flowering.
- **Chocolate Vine (C2):** after flowering, prune hard if needed. It is very vigorous and needs at least one hard prune a year.
- **Clematis montana (C3, Group 1) †:** prune after flowering, in late April or May.
- **Epimedium (S7), Hellebore (S10):** finish tidying. Hellebore gets its annual compost mulch after flowering (guide).
- **Hops (C9) †:** train the new shoots up the support.

**Feed and mulch**
- **Ericaceous liquid feed from April to August:** Blueberry (F5, fortnightly), Cranberry (F6, monthly), Lingonberry (F13, monthly). Heathers (G6, an ericaceous fertiliser in March or April, or a liquid feed such as Phostrogen April to September). Use only ericaceous feeds, and no general-purpose feeds on cranberry.
- **Monthly liquid feeds start:** Hosta (S11, April through midsummer), Japanese Maple in containers (T25, April to July), and Clematis (C3: high-potash tomato feed fortnightly from April to August).
- **Slow-release spring feeds** (if not yet done): Japanese Maple (T25), Hosta (S11), Hydrangea (T32), Magnolias (T30), Camellia (T22, after flowering).
- **Hydrangea macrophylla (T32):** one spring feed. For blue flowers use a feed low in phosphorus and high in potassium, with ericaceous compost. Stop feeding after August.
- **Bark mulch** 5–7 cm around established plants, once the soil is warm and moist. Keep it off stems and crowns. †

**Protect**
- **Frost fleece on blossom** at night (peach, plum, cherry, pear: F15, F8, F17, F24, F26, F16). Remove it by day so pollinators can reach the flowers. †
- **Slugs and snails:** peak danger for Hosta (S11), Lupins (P26), Delphiniums (P12 †).
- **Tomato and tender plant hardening:** begin hardening off half-hardy annuals and tomatoes in a sheltered spot by day. †

**Harvest and enjoy**
- **Rhubarb (F21) †:** pull stems from established crowns, but not in the first year after planting.
- **Wild Garlic (H32):** leaves and flowers from April to June. Check identity by crushing a leaf: it must smell strongly of garlic, because the leaves can be confused with Lily of the Valley and Lords-and-Ladies.
- **Scurvy Grass (H30), Common Sorrel (H7), Chives (H5):** snip leaves sparingly.
- **In flower:** Tulips (B28), Fritillaria meleagris (B12), Grape Hyacinth (B13), Bluebells (B19, late April), Wood Anemone (B3), Primulas (S17), Forget-me-nots (S8), Bleeding Heart (S1), Lungwort (P25), Magnolias (T30), Camellia (T22), Skimmia (T26) and Hawthorn blossom (T7, May).

**Leave alone.** Let daffodil, bluebell, tulip and other bulb foliage yellow naturally. Daffodil foliage needs at least six weeks before removal (guide).

**Pond and bog.** Marsh Marigold (W17) in flower. Avoid disturbing the pond bed while frogspawn is present. †

**If it is too wet.** Pot up in the greenhouse or porch, hold off planting out, and put fleece or cloches over sown rows.

### MAY

**Galway conditions.** Mean about 11–12 °C, with mild days (highs about 15 °C). Frost risk is nearly over on the coast and falling inland, but cold nights remain possible, so keep fleece until the last week of the month. Wind is still a constant.

**Priorities:** (1) planting out tender plants after hardening off, (2) sowing biennials and successional crops, (3) supports, and (4) slug control at its peak.

**Plant out (after frost risk)**
- **Patio Tomatoes (H23):** plant out in mid-May on the coast (the guide's date for most of Ireland), or late May inland and in exposed spots †. Bury the stem to the lowest pair of leaves. Choose a sheltered, sunny spot under eaves or by a warm wall, which also cuts blight risk.
- **Courgettes (H33) and Cucumbers (H9):** plant out after all frost risk has passed. Outdoor ridge cucumbers cope reasonably but crop best in a sheltered, sunny spot or a polytunnel.
- **Half-hardy annuals:** Cockscomb (A4), Globe Amaranth (A8), Lambs Tails (A100), Strawflower (A17), Zinnia (A21, needs a warm, sheltered spot and good airflow to avoid mildew), Snapdragons (A16), Mealycup Sage (A12 †), Slipperwort (A2 †).
- **Tender perennials and pots:** Lemon Verbena (H17), Lemongrass (H18, needs at least 13 °C), Pineapple Lily (B21), Dwarf Sword Lily (B9), Iron Cross (B15). Move Egyptian Papyrus (W8) and Umbrella Grass (W22) back outdoors late in the month.
- **Basil (H2):** only on a warm patio or in a greenhouse.

**Sow outdoors**
- **Cosmos (A6)** once frost risk has passed, **Nasturtiums (H20)**, **Zinnia** direct only in a warm spot, **Sweet Alyssum (A18)**, **Borage (A1)**, **Phacelia (A10)** in successional sowings.
- **Salads and roots, little and often:** Radish, Rocket, Spring Onions, Mustard Greens, Garden Cress, Coriander, Dill, Parsley, Peas (H26, H27, H101, H100, H12, H8, H11, H22, H24).
- **Biennials and short-lived perennials for next year:** Honesty (P18, late spring to summer), Forget-me-not seed (S8, late spring to summer), Campanula patula (P4, late spring to summer), Foxglove (S9 †, May to July).

**Supports and tying in.** Stake Delphiniums (P12) and Lupins (P26) †, tall Cosmos (A6) and Snapdragons (A16) (guide: wind), Sweet Peas (A19) on canes or a wigwam, Peas (H24), Hops (C9), Kiwi (C11), Wisteria (C16) and Passion Flower (C1). Tie in new Blackberry canes (F3) as they grow.

**Prune and tidy**
- **Clematis montana (C3, Group 1):** finish pruning after flowering. †
- **Lilac (T29) †:** deadhead and thin after flowering.
- **Lungwort (P25) †:** cut back after flowering to refresh foliage.
- **Candytuft (G5):** shear back by about a third straight after flowering for a compact second flush.
- **Rosemary (H28):** trim lightly after flowering and never into old bare wood. †
- **Bleeding Heart (S1):** goes dormant by midsummer. Mark its position so you do not dig it up.
- **Verbena bonariensis (P101):** pinch back young stems in early summer for a bushier plant, and deadhead if self-seeding gets out of hand.

**Feed**
- **Kiwi (C11):** high-potash liquid feed fortnightly from May to August.
- **Apple (F2):** high-potash liquid feed fortnightly from late spring to midsummer.
- **Strawberries (F25):** high-potash fortnightly from first flowers until fruiting ends.
- **Raspberry (F19):** high-potash fortnightly from bud break until fruit set.
- **Plum 'Aprimira' (F17, in pots):** potash-rich granular feed in May and again in July.
- **Roses (T36, C6):** high-potash liquid fortnightly from May to August (the second granular feed is in early June).
- **Wisteria (C16):** high-potash feed (tomato feed) from spring through early summer.
- **Delphiniums (P12):** high-potash fortnightly from bud formation.
- **Peonies (P31):** high-potash feed as buds develop.
- **Honeysuckle (C4), Jasmine (C7), Passion Flower (C1):** high-potash through summer for flowers.
- **Strawflower, Zinnia, Cockscomb, Gomphrena:** monthly balanced liquid feed through the growing season.

**Hedges and the Wildlife Act.** No hedge cutting. Delay any work that would disturb nesting birds.

**Harvest and enjoy.** Rhubarb (F21, stop pulling by about midsummer †). Asparagus (H1) in year three or later only. Spring onions, radishes, rocket, cress, chives, wild garlic flowers. In flower: Allium (B20), Camassia (B4), Peonies (P31), Siberian Iris (P19, May to June), Lupins (P26), Columbine (P8), Geum (P2, P7), Globeflower (P17), Wisteria (C16) and Hawthorn (T7).

**Pond and bog.** The best time to plant most pond plants runs from late April to June. † Plant Water Lilies (W26) in baskets: set the rhizome level with the top of lime-free aquatic compost, top with lime-free pea gravel, start shallow and lower gradually as leaves grow. Keep lilies away from fountains and pumps. Push in fresh aquatic fertiliser tablets (never loose fertiliser, which causes algae). Lotus (W15) only once the water is warm. Plant Marsh Orchids (W18) from nursery-propagated stock only.

**If it is too wet.** Hold tender plants back in the porch or greenhouse, and protect from slugs. A cold, wet May means planting out late, not planting out into mud.

### JUNE

**Galway conditions.** The longest days of the year, mean about 13–14 °C. Showers can be heavy (Athenry recorded 206% of its average June rainfall in 2025), but dry spells also occur. Pots dry out surprisingly quickly in warm, windy weather.

**Priorities:** (1) deadheading and cutting back, (2) the second rose feed, (3) summer sowing of biennials, and (4) watching for blight, mildew and slugs.

**Sow and plant**
- **Sweet William (P35):** sow June to July as a biennial for flowers next year.
- **Honesty (P18), Forget-me-nots (S8), Foxglove (S9 †), Campanula patula (P4):** sow now for flowers next year.
- **Parsley (H22) and winter salad crops †:** sow now for autumn and winter.
- **Last sowing of Peas (H24)** in early summer.
- **Successions:** Radish, Rocket, Spring Onions, Mustard Greens, Coriander, Dill, Garden Cress. Rocket bolts quickly in dry or hot spells, so keep it watered and pick young leaves.
- **Phacelia (A10)** as a green manure between crops.
- **Norfolk Island Pine (T34):** move outside only once frost risk has fully passed in early summer, to a sheltered, sunny position.

**Feed**
- **Roses (T36, C6):** the second specialist rose feed in early June.
- **Tomatoes (H23):** weekly tomato feed once the first fruits set. Courgettes (H33) and Cucumbers (H9): weekly high-potash feed once fruiting begins.
- **Sweet Peas (A19):** weekly balanced feed once flowering begins, switching to tomato feed for more flowers.
- **Do not feed** Wisteria (C16) after midsummer.

**Prune and tidy**
- **Mock Orange (T28), Deutzia (T15), Lilac (T29 †):** prune straight after flowering. On Deutzia, remove a proportion of the oldest stems at the base each year.
- **Lupins (P26):** cut back after flowering for a possible second flush.
- **Delphiniums (P12):** cut spent stems and give a balanced liquid feed for a second flush.
- **Columbine (P8), Geum (P2, P7), Bellflowers (P4, P102), Catnip (H3):** deadhead or trim after flowering to keep them neat and extend flowering.
- **Alstroemeria (P1):** pull (rather than cut) spent flowering stems to encourage more.
- **Thyme (H10):** trim after flowering to keep it bushy and stop it becoming woody.
- **Mint (H19, H25):** cut back after flowering and feed.
- **Kiwi (C11):** summer prune. Shorten laterals to 4–6 leaves beyond the last fruit cluster.
- **Fig (F7):** pinch out growing tips in early summer to encourage the embryo fruit that overwinter and ripen next year.
- **Redcurrant, Gooseberry (F20, F9) †:** shorten this year's side shoots in late June or July.
- **Apple (F2) †:** if heavily laden, thin fruitlets after the natural June drop.
- **Blackberry (F3) and Raspberry (F19):** keep tying in new canes.

**Lift and dry**
- **Garden-hybrid Tulips (B28):** if you lift them (common in Ireland's wet climate), do so when the foliage has died back in early summer. Dry them off and store until November replanting.
- **Daffodils (B7), Bluebells (B19):** foliage must die back naturally.

**Harvest and enjoy.** Strawberries (F25, from June), Woodland Strawberry (F27, June to autumn), Garlic if leaves yellow early (H13), Chives (H5), Rocket and Spring Onions. Alpine and rockery plants in flower: Lewisia (G19), Saxifrage (G18), St Patrick's Cabbage (S22, early summer), Creeping Thyme (G20). Climbing and border: Climbing Hydrangea (C5, from about June †), Honeysuckle (C4), Roses, Elder 'Black Lace' (T11), Lupins, Astrantia (P27), Daylilies (P11) and the first Lilies (B16). Jasmine (C7) follows later in summer.

**Pests and diseases**
- **Tomato blight** thrives in cool, damp weather (guide): keep tomatoes under eaves or a roof, water at the base, and remove affected leaves promptly. †
- **Powdery mildew on Zinnia (A21):** give airflow and avoid overcrowding.
- **Slugs and snails:** keep protecting Hostas, Lupins and Delphiniums.
- **Vine weevil in pots †:** check for notched leaf edges.

**Water.** Check pots and raised beds, which dry first. Water the crown of tree ferns (S23, S18, S20) in dry spells: the guide says drying out is the most common cause of failure. Water the roots of Blueberry (F5) with rainwater where you can. †

**Pond and bog.** Skim blanket weed. † Do not disturb Water Violet (W29) in summer. Deadhead Yellow Flag Iris (W25) before seeds scatter (it spreads by seed and rhizome). Thin vigorous plants such as Water Mint (W27), Mare's Tail (W16), Water Soldier (W28) if grown, and Loosestrife (W14).

**If it is too wet.** Provide shelter for tomatoes and tender fruit, deadhead promptly, pick strawberries before they rot, and watch for slugs.

### JULY

**Galway conditions.** The warmest month, with a mean of about 15–16 °C and highs of 18–19 °C. It can still be wet (Athenry had 144% of its average July rainfall in 2025), and heat spikes do happen (Connemara National Park reached 32.2 °C on 13 July 2025). Watch for both drought stress in pots and fungal disease after rain.

**Priorities:** (1) summer pruning of stone fruit, (2) harvesting, (3) the last feeds of the year for many plants, and (4) ordering autumn bulbs.

**Prune and tidy**
- **Wisteria (C16):** cut the whippy green shoots back to five or six leaves, in July or August (RHS). †
- **Sweet Cherry (F26):** prune straight after harvest. **Sour Cherry (F24):** after harvest, cut out some of the older fruited wood, since it fruits on the previous year's growth.
- **Plum (F8, F17), Peach (F15), Almond (F1):** if pruning is needed, do it in summer only.
- **Climbing Honeysuckle (C4) †:** tidy after flowering.
- **Schisandra (C13):** prune lightly after flowering and tie in young shoots. Avoid hard pruning.
- **Redcurrant and Gooseberry (F20, F9) †:** finish shortening side shoots.
- **Blackberry (F3):** keep tying in new canes.
- **Deadhead** Cosmos (A6), Pot Marigold (A15), Zinnia (A21), Snapdragons (A16), Daylilies (P11), Geum (P2, P7), Coreopsis (P22), Scabiosa (P34). Pick **Sweet Peas (A19)** regularly, because letting pods form ends flowering.
- **Verbena bonariensis (P101) and Nigella (A11):** deadhead if you do not want self-seeded plants everywhere.
- **Cut back** Meadowsweet (W19) after flowering, and Ragged Robin (W20) too, to keep them tidy.

**Sow and plant**
- **Sweet William (P35):** last sowings in June to July for next year.
- **Honesty (P18), Forget-me-nots (S8), Foxglove (S9 †):** sow for next year if not done.
- **Salad crops:** Radish, Rocket, Spring Onions, Mustard Greens, Parsley and Garden Cress, little and often.
- **Colchicum (B18):** plant corms 7–10 cm deep, from July to August. Wear gloves: all parts are highly toxic. Protect the corms and buds from slugs with grit or copper tape.
- **Order spring bulbs now** for the best choice (daffodils, tulips, alliums, fritillarias, crocus).
- **Bearded Iris (P19) †:** divide and replant after flowering. Plant rhizomes level with the soil surface, top exposed to the sun and not buried. Do this every 3–4 years, discarding the old woody centre. Give them a raised, gritty, south-facing bed.

**Feed (last feeds)**
- **Apple (F2):** no feeding after July. **Plum 'Aprimira' (F17, in pots):** potash-rich granular feed in July.
- **Japanese Maple (T25):** last liquid feed in July. **Hosta (S11):** feed to midsummer.
- **Roses (T36, C6):** fortnightly high-potash through August.
- **Tomatoes (H23), Courgettes (H33), Cucumbers (H9):** weekly high-potash feed.
- **Strawberries (F25):** continue tomato feed until fruiting ends.
- **Blueberry, Cranberry, Lingonberry (F5, F6, F13):** ericaceous feed continues to August.
- **Do not feed** Wisteria (C16), Plum (F8, F17) or Peach (F15) after midsummer. They need to ripen their wood before winter.

**Harvest**
- **Berries and fruit:** Strawberries (F25), Woodland Strawberry (F27), Raspberries (F19, summer-fruiting), Blackcurrants, Redcurrants and Gooseberries (F4, F20, F9 †), Sour Cherry (F24), and the first **Lingonberry crop (F13, late July)**.
- **Courgettes (H33):** pick every 2–3 days while young to keep plants cropping.
- **Garlic (H13):** lift when the leaves begin to yellow. Cure the bulbs somewhere airy before storing.
- **Peas (H24):** pick pods regularly.
- **Herbs:** harvest Lavender (H15) for drying as it opens. Pick Chives, Mint, Lemon Balm, Oregano, Basil and Sage regularly.
- **For drying:** Statice and Sea Lavender (P24, P37), Strawflower (A17), Globe Amaranth (A8), Cockscomb (A4), Drumstick Flower (A7), Winged Everlasting (A20), Chinese Lantern (P100, in autumn).

**Water.** Check pots and raised beds daily in a hot spell. Water Wisteria and fruit trees in dry weeks. Water the crown of tree ferns (S23, S18, S20) regularly.

**Pests and diseases.** Blight on tomatoes after humid rain. Mildew on Zinnia (A21) and roses. † Slugs after every shower.

**Pond and bog.** Top up with rainwater where possible. † Thin vigorous plants: Water Mint (W27), Mare's Tail (W16), Water Soldier (W28), Loosestrife (W14), Yellow Flag Iris (W25). Leave Water Violet (W29) undisturbed.

**Hedges.** Still no cutting until September.

**If it is too wet.** Harvest fruit before it splits or rots, keep tomato foliage dry, and pick Sweet Peas, courgettes and beans before they overrun.

### AUGUST

**Galway conditions.** Mean about 15 °C. August can be very wet (Athenry had 276% of its average in August 2019) or dry. Nights lengthen and the soil is still warm, so autumn planting preparation can begin.

**Priorities:** (1) the last summer pruning, (2) stopping feeds, (3) harvesting, and (4) planting autumn-flowering bulbs.

**Prune and tidy**
- **Lavender (H15):** trim lightly after flowering and never into old bare wood. Do the same for **Sage (H29)** once it has flowered. †
- **Wisteria (C16):** finish the summer prune by mid-August.
- **Blackberries (F3):** cut fruited canes back after harvest and tie in the new ones.
- **Raspberries, summer-fruiting (F19) †:** cut fruited canes to the ground straight after harvest and tie in the strongest new canes, six to eight per plant.
- **Blackcurrant (F4):** feed again after harvest with a high-nitrogen fertiliser (guide). Prune out older fruited stems after harvest or in winter. †
- **Hazelnut (F11):** "brutting": snap over long side shoots in late summer to improve nut set.
- **Peach, Plum, Cherry, Almond (F15, F8, F17, F26, F24, F1):** last chance for summer pruning, if needed.
- **Common Jasmine (C7):** prune immediately after flowering, thinning crowded and dead growth. Do not cut hard in late winter or spring, because it flowers on the current year's growth.
- **Deadhead** Cosmos, Calendula, Penstemon (P30), Helenium (P39), Coneflower (P9, leave the late heads for winter birds) and Alstroemeria (P1).
- **Let some seed ripen** on Borage (A1), Cornflower (A5), Calendula (A15), Honesty (P18) and Quaking Grass (A9), and collect seed for sowing. Self-seeders will return on their own.

**Sow and plant**
- **Colchicum (B18)** corms until the end of the month, with gloves.
- **Autumn-flowering Crocus, *C. speciosus* and *C. sativus* (B6):** plant 8–10 cm deep, a little earlier than the spring types.
- **Cyclamen coum (B27):** plant tubers 2–3 cm deep, flat side down, from August to September.
- **Salad crops for autumn:** Radish, Rocket, Mustard Greens, Spring Onions, Garden Cress and Parsley, sown in short rows. † Rocket and Mustard Greens grow well in the cooler, damper weather that follows.
- **Hardy annuals for earlier flowers next year:** Cornflower (A5, autumn sowing allowed), Love-in-a-Mist (A11), Poppies (A14), Pot Marigold (A15) and Poached Egg Plant (A13). The guide gives spring *or autumn* for these. † Late August to September is a good time on well-drained ground.
- **New strawberry plants †** can go in from late summer.

**Feed: stop these**
- **Roses (T36, C6):** the last feed by late August.
- **Clematis (C3):** no feeding after August.
- **Kiwi (C11), Hosta (S11), Hydrangea (T32):** no feeding after August.

**Harvest**
- **Peach (F15):** ripens early to mid-August in a warm spot.
- **Plum 'Aprimira' (F17):** August to September.
- **Blackberries, Raspberries, Lingonberry (F3, F19, F13):** keep picking.
- **Early Apples and Pears (F2, F16)** in the warmest seasons. †
- **Tomatoes, Cucumbers, Courgettes (H23, H9, H33):** peak cropping.
- **Figs (F7) †:** ripen from the embryo fruit formed last year.
- **Cut flowers and dried flowers:** Statice, Sea Holly (P36), Globe Thistle (P16) and the everlastings listed under July.

**Water.** Keep newly planted trees, shrubs and containers watered in dry spells. The guide stresses the crown of tree ferns (S23, S18, S20).

**Pond and bog.** Thin vigorous plants and remove excess Hornwort (W13) if the pond is crowded. † Start planning for leaf-fall netting.

**Hedges.** Not yet. Wait for 1 September.

**If it is too wet.** Pick fruit early and use it, keep blight and mildew under control, and delay sowing in waterlogged ground.

### SEPTEMBER

**Galway conditions.** Mean about 13–14 °C. Rain returns but the soil is still warm, making this one of the **best planting months** for clay gardens: roots establish before winter and the soil is easy to work. Equinoctial gales can strip trees and fruit. **Hedge cutting is permitted again from 1 September.**

**Priorities:** (1) start planting bulbs, (2) plant and divide hardy perennials, shrubs and trees, (3) cut hedges, and (4) harvest the main fruit.

**Plant bulbs (all from the guide's autumn timings)**
- **Daffodils (B7):** 15 cm deep, September to October. Naturalised drifts get a compost mulch each autumn.
- **Snowdrops (B25), dry bulbs:** 10 cm deep and 8–10 cm apart, September to October.
- **Anemones, including Wood Anemone (B3):** 5–8 cm deep in September to October. Soak the corms for 24 hours first.
- **Crocus, spring types (B6):** September to November, 7–10 cm deep.
- **Lilies (B16) and Martagon Lily (B17):** 15 cm deep on a bed of grit, September to October.
- **Winter Aconite (B29):** 5 cm deep in early autumn while the soil is warm.
- **Erythronium (B10):** 10 cm deep, in autumn while the soil is still warm. Do not plant in spring.
- **Camassia (B4):** 10–15 cm. **Grape Hyacinth (B13), Siberian Squill (B24), Hyacinth (B14), Dwarf Iris (B8):** 8–10 cm. **Native Bluebells (B19):** 8 cm. **Yellow Star-of-Bethlehem (B30):** 5–8 cm. **Snowflake (B26):** 10 cm in moist, humus-rich soil.
- **Fritillaria (B12):** plant promptly after buying, because small bulbs dry out quickly. *F. meleagris* 10 cm, *F. imperialis* 20–30 cm on its side, *F. persica* 15–20 cm on its side. The tricky species want a raised bed or gritty container.
- **Lily of the Valley (S12):** plant pips 2–3 cm deep. **Wild Garlic (H32):** bulbs 2.5 cm deep in a contained woodland corner.
- **Hold back:** Alliums (mid to late autumn, October) and garden hybrid Tulips (November).
- **Cyclamen coum (B27):** last planting of dormant tubers this month.

**Plant and divide, using the clay recipe**
- **Container-grown trees, shrubs and perennials †:** warm soil plus autumn rain is ideal. Include Magnolias (T30), Hydrangea (T32), Camellia (T22), Skimmia (T26), Hellebore (S10), Epimedium (S7) and Japanese Anemone (P20).
- **Peonies (P31) †:** plant or divide in early autumn. Keep the eyes no more than 2.5–5 cm below the soil (too deep means no flowers). Tree peony graft union 10–15 cm below the surface.
- **Siberian Iris (P19):** plant at soil level or 2–3 cm below, in spring or early autumn.
- **Solomon's Seal (S21):** rhizome 5–8 cm deep, autumn or spring.
- **Divide** Hostas (S11), Daylilies (P11), Heuchera (S4), Lungwort (P25, every 3–4 years), Chinese Lantern (P100, spring or autumn, into a root barrier) and Bergenia (P14 †).
- **Evergreen shrubs and conifers †:** planting in early autumn lets them root in warm soil before winter.

**Prune and tidy**
- **Hedges (T7, T18, T20, T21):** trim from now. Confirm no nests are in use first. Wear thick gloves for Barberry (T21).
- **Climbers:** tie in Wisteria (C16), Clematis (C3), Passion Flower (C1), Kiwi (C11) and Roses (C6) before the autumn gales. †
- **Hops (C9) †:** cut back bines when they die back, in autumn to winter.
- **Chinese Lantern (P100):** cut stems as the lanterns turn orange, strip the leaves and hang them upside down to dry. Wear gloves.

**Sow**
- **Hardy annuals** (see August): Cornflower, Love-in-a-Mist, Poppies, Calendula, Poached Egg Plant.
- **Salad crops under cover:** Rocket, Mustard Greens, Garden Cress, Radish. †
- **Garlic (H13):** buy cloves now for planting in October to November.

**Harvest**
- **Apples (F2) and Pears (F16):** from late summer into October, depending on variety.
- **Plums (F8, F17) and Blackberries (F3):** end of the season.
- **Lingonberry (F13):** second crop in late September.
- **Sea Buckthorn (F22):** orange berries in autumn. Wear gloves for the thorns.
- **Hazelnuts (F11):** squirrels are usually quicker than you.
- **Elderberries from 'Black Lace' (T11):** edible when cooked.
- **Grapes (C8) and Kiwi (C11):** in a good year, September to November. †
- **Autumn Raspberries (F19).**
- **Green tomatoes †:** ripen them indoors if blight or cold arrives.

**Mulch and soil.** Spread compost mulch on naturalised daffodil drifts and snowdrops (guide). Mulch Climbing Hydrangea (C5) in late summer or autumn. Top-dress Cyclamen (B27) with leaf mould. Start collecting fallen leaves for leaf mould. †

**Pond and bog.** Net the pond before the leaves fall. † Trim overgrown marginals. Hornwort (W13) will form winter buds that sink to the bottom (guide).

**Tender plants.** Prepare space under cover for pots that must come in next month (see October).

**If it is too wet.** Plant in the gaps between showers, but only when the clay crumbles. Use raised beds and grit-rich mixes where the soil stays soggy.

### OCTOBER

**Galway conditions.** Mean about 11 °C, with frequent rain, gales and falling leaves. Inland, the first air frost can arrive this month, while coastal gardens usually stay frost-free until December. This is the key month for **bringing tender plants in** and for planting the last bulbs.

**Priorities:** (1) rescue tender plants before the first frost, (2) plant alliums and garlic, (3) lift tender bulbs, and (4) mulch and tidy.

**Bring in or protect (before the first frost)**
- **Pots to move under cover:** Lemon Verbena (H17, keep on the dry side), Lemongrass (H18), Norfolk Island Pine (T34, bright and cool at about 17 °C, with no feeding over winter), Slipperwort (A2), Pickle Plant (G14), Cushion Bush (T8), and container-grown Olive (F14), Pomegranate (F18), Golden Mimosa (T17), Agapanthus (B2), Society Garlic (P41) and Pineapple Lily (B21).
- **Egyptian Papyrus (W8) and Umbrella Grass (W22):** lift and move to a frost-free place.
- **Tree ferns (S23, S18, S20):** prepare fleece or straw for crown wrapping. Wrap in November, or earlier if a frost is forecast.
- **Overwintering wet-sensitive plants** (Rosemary H28, Lavender H15, Sage H29, French Tarragon H31 in pots): move pots to a sheltered, dry spot. The guide says these herbs struggle most with winter wet on clay.

**Lift and store**
- **Acidanthera (B1):** lift after the first frosts, dry and store frost-free.
- **Freesia (B11), Ixia (B5), Iron Cross (B15), Ranunculus (B22):** lift after the foliage dies down and store dry and frost-free, or keep the pots dry under cover.
- **Dwarf Sword Lily (B9):** in colder, more exposed spots lift when the foliage dies back. In milder gardens a thick dry mulch is often enough.
- **Pineapple Lily (B21):** lift, or mulch thickly if left in the ground.

**Plant**
- **Alliums (B20):** plant mid to late autumn, 2–3 times the bulb's depth (about 20 cm for large bulbs, 8–10 cm for small ones). Alliums do very well in pots on wet clay.
- **Last daffodils, lilies and snowdrops (B7, B16, B25)**, and species tulips if you want them in earlier than the main tulip planting (B28).
- **Garlic (H13):** plant cloves 2.5 cm deep (up to 5 cm in light soil), pointed end up, in October to November. On heavy clay it is often more reliable to start cloves in modules in a cold frame and plant out in early spring.
- **Sweet Peas (A19):** sow in pots under cover for earlier, stronger plants next year.
- **Container-grown trees, shrubs and perennials †:** still a good window while the soil is warm and moist.
- **Heather (G6), Hellebore (S10), Epimedium (S7), Hepatica (S19)** and other woodland plants: autumn planting suits them. †

**Prune and tidy**
- **Asparagus (H1):** cut back the ferny growth when it turns yellow, then top-dress with compost.
- **Hops (C9) †:** cut the bines back once they have died down.
- **Fig (F7) †:** remove any fruit larger than a pea. Keep the pea-sized embryo figs, which survive winter and ripen next year.
- **Leave seed heads standing** on Coneflower (P9, for seed-eating birds), Border Sedum (P6, structure), Feather Reed Grass (P28) and Statice for winter interest.
- **Cut back** collapsed foliage and diseased leaves (rose blackspot, mildewed growth). † Do not compost badly diseased material.
- **Cut back** Meadowsweet (W19) and other spent bog plants if you did not do so earlier.
- **Climbing and shrub roses (C6, T36) †:** shorten very long stems to reduce wind rock before the gales.

**Mulch**
- **Rhubarb (F21):** generous well-rotted manure or compost mulch after the leaves die back. The guide calls this the most beneficial treatment.
- **Fruit bushes:** compost or manure mulch around Blackcurrant (F4) and Redcurrant (F20). Keep it clear of the stems.
- **Daffodil drifts (B7), Snowdrops (B25), Cyclamen coum (B27):** compost or leaf mould mulch.
- **Bamboo (T3):** top-dress with compost each autumn (guide).
- **Leaves:** gather them for leaf mould. Keep leaf litter off native mosses (S15), which it smothers, and off the crowns of small plants. †

**Harvest.** Apples and Pears (F2, F16), Hazelnuts (F11), Sea Buckthorn (F22), Rowan berries (T33, for the birds), Autumn Raspberries (F19), Hardy Grapes (C8), Kiwi (C11 †), Horseradish roots (H102 †, dig after the leaves die back) and Cranberry (F6 †).

**Enjoy** Japanese Anemone (P20), Toad Lily (S24), Monkshood (S13, keep away from children and pets), Meadow Saffron (B18), autumn Crocus (B6), Hydrangea (T32), Japanese Maple (T25), Ginkgo (T16), Virginia Creeper (C15) and Rowan (T33) for autumn colour, and Chinese Lantern (P100).

**Pond and bog.** Keep the net in place. † Cut back dead marginal foliage, but leave some for wildlife. Arrowhead (W1) goes dormant. Hornwort (W13) and Frogbit (W11) sink as winter buds.

**If it is too wet.** Pot up bulbs in a shed or porch. Bulbs in pots on feet, in gritty compost, cope much better with a wet autumn than bulbs in sodden clay.

---

### NOVEMBER

**Galway conditions.** Mean about 8 °C, short and very wet days, and gales. Clear nights bring frost. Clay is heavy and sticky. This is the start of the bare-root season, but only on days when the soil is not waterlogged.

**Priorities:** (1) plant tulips and bare-root fruit trees, (2) start winter pruning, (3) protect tender plants and check stakes before the gales, and (4) clean up.

**Plant**
- **Garden hybrid Tulips (B28):** plant in November, 20 cm deep (three times the bulb's height), which lowers the risk of tulip fire. Species tulips can go in a little earlier. For pots, the guide's reference table suggests a 25–30 cm container of 10 L or more. Plan to lift hybrids after the foliage dies back next summer, or treat them as annuals.
- **Garlic (H13):** finish planting by the end of the month.
- **Bare-root fruit trees and bushes †:** plant when the clay is workable. Do not plant into standing water.
  - **Apple, Plum, Pear (F2, F8, F16):** graft union at or just above soil level, planted to the nursery soil mark. Stake permanently.
  - **Blackcurrant (F4):** plant 5–7 cm deeper than the nursery mark, then cut all stems to 10 cm.
  - **Gooseberry (F9):** roots covered by about 5 cm, then cut all stems to 30 cm.
  - **Redcurrant (F20):** roots covered by about 5 cm.
  - **Raspberry (F19):** crown 5–7 cm below soil, then cut canes to 25 cm.
  - **Roses (T36, C6):** graft union just below soil level.
  - **Hawthorn hedging whips (T7) †:** a cheap, wind-hardy shelterbelt.
- **Heel in** bare-root plants in a trench of free-draining soil if the clay is too wet to plant. †

**Prune and tidy**
- **Apple (F2) † and Pear (F16):** winter pruning can begin now, on dry days.
- **Redcurrant, Gooseberry, Blackcurrant (F20, F9, F4) †:** winter pruning from now to February.
- **Climbing roses (C6) †:** prune and tie in.
- **Do not prune** *Prunus* (guide).
- **Clear** leaves from borders, pond and paths, and remove any diseased growth. †

**Protect**
- **Wrap tree ferns (S23, S18, S20)** with fleece or straw around the crown if hard frost is forecast. Check that wrapping does not stay wet.
- **Mulch tender perennials** where left in the ground: River Lily (B23, dry winter mulch), Alstroemeria (P1, dry winter mulch), Agapanthus (B2), Pineapple Lily (B21).
- **Check all stakes and ties** on young trees, dwarf-rootstock fruit trees, Black Locust (T4, brittle in wind) and tall climbers. Tie in loose growth. †
- **Pots outdoors:** group them against a wall, raise them on feet, and wrap if frost is forecast. †
- **Slugs** remain active in mild spells, so protect emerging bulb shoots and tender growth. †

**Mulch.** Apply bark mulch 5–7 cm deep to new plantings, keeping it off stems and crowns. Apply compost or manure mulch to fruit bushes and rhubarb if not already done.

**Harvest.** Kiwi (C11 †), late Apples and Pears, Horseradish (H102 †), Parsley, winter salad crops under cover, and the last Hardy Grapes.

**Enjoy.** Autumn colour, winter berries (Skimmia T26, Prickly Heath G15, Holly T18), and the first Winter Heath (G6) and Witch Hazel (T41) flowers.

**Pond and bog.** Remove the net once the leaves have fallen. Remove dead foliage and any rotting debris from the bottom. † Do not remove all vegetation, because the pond needs overwintering cover.

**Tools and kit †.** Clean and oil tools, wash and store spare pots, and sort grit, bark and compost stocks.

**If it is too wet.** Heel in, sort and clean, and prune on dry days. Do not struggle with a waterlogged bed.

---

### DECEMBER

**Galway conditions.** Mean about 6–7 °C. Along with January, one of the two wettest months, with the shortest days and strong gales. The clay is at its heaviest.

**Priorities:** (1) look after the garden's structure and protection, (2) plan, and (3) enjoy winter interest.

**Plant (only if the clay is workable)**
- **Bare-root fruit trees, bushes and roses †:** as for November. Do not plant in waterlogged or frozen ground. Heel plants in until conditions improve.
- **Last Tulips (B28):** if still unplanted and the soil is workable.

**Prune and tidy**
- **Hardy Grapes (C8) †:** prune now while dormant, from December to January.
- **Apple (F2) † and Pear (F16):** winter pruning on dry, frost-free days.
- **Currants and Gooseberries (F4, F20, F9) †.**
- **Never prune stone fruit** (Almond, Plum, Peach, cherries): summer only.

**Protect and check**
- **Tree fern crowns (S23, S18, S20)** wrapped and dry. Check again after each frost.
- **Stakes and ties** after each gale.
- **Overwintering pots under cover:** Lemon Verbena (H17), Lemongrass (H18), Norfolk Island Pine (T34, bright, about 17 °C, no feeding), and any Olive, Pomegranate and Golden Mimosa in containers. Water sparingly.
- **Stored bulbs and tubers (B1, B5, B9, B11, B15, B22):** inspect for rot or drying out. †
- **Snow or wet weight on conifers and evergreens †:** shake it off gently.

**Pond.** Keep a small hole in any ice, using warm water. Do not smash the ice. †

**Christmas greenery.** Holly (T18) berries are toxic, so keep sprigs away from children and pets. Norfolk Island Pine (T34) makes a good indoor "Christmas tree" if kept bright and cool. In the garden, the guide calls Balsam Fir (T2) the archetypal Christmas tree and Fraser Fir (T14) a fragrant conifer specimen.

**Enjoy.** Witch Hazel (T41), Winter Heath (G6), Hellebore (S10), Skimmia (T26) and Prickly Heath (G15) berries, Cyclamen coum (B27), early Snowdrops (B25), Holly (T18) and Ivy (C10).

**Plan.** Review what worked. Note where water stood and which plants suffered, then plan raised beds, grit or containers for those spots next year. Order seeds and tubers.

**If it is too wet.** Stay indoors. The garden will wait.

## 7. Materials by season (using what the guide lists)

| Material | When it matters most |
|---|---|
| **Horticultural grit** | Autumn and spring planting (20–30% into the clay mix). Spring top-dressing for pasqueflower, houseleek, lewisia and thyme. Lily beds in September. |
| **Ericaceous compost** | Spring: top-dress blueberry (fresh compost each spring), and pot up cranberry, lingonberry, camellia and skimmia. |
| **Horticultural bark** | April mulching (5–7 cm). Autumn top-up. Pine bark for lingonberry and heathers. |
| **Perlite and pebbles** | Spring repotting and pot drainage (10–20% perlite; pebbles at the base). Autumn: re-check that pots drain freely before winter. |
| **Vermiculite** | Seed sowing, February to May. |
| **Multipurpose compost** | Planting-hole mix (50/50 with clay) in autumn and spring. Mulches in autumn. |
